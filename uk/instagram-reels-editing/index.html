Five signs of a Reel that was edited properly

It was composed for the screen

Instagram draws the username, caption and buttons over the video. A properly edited Reel keeps the face, the product and the captions clear of them.

The opening does the work

No logo sting and no 'hi everyone'. The Reel opens on the result, the surprise or the question, and the same first frame doubles as your grid cover.

The captions look like yours

Typeface, weight, colour and highlight style are agreed up front and reused, so your Reels read as one account.

Cuts and movement feel native

Short, purposeful cuts and gentle zooms feel at home on Instagram. Over-produced effects look like adverts and tend to be skipped.

The audio was mixed

Voices are levelled, music sits under them and effects are kept light. You supply any trending audio you want, since licensing is yours.

The monthly rhythm

You start with a 30-minute call about your Reels and your look, and the delivery schedule is set there. After that, clips go into the shared folder when they exist. UK clients often upload after each shoot day, which spreads the editing over the month and means the style can be corrected before it repeats.

Reels arrive for review, each with 2 revisions included. Approved Reels are finished files that you schedule yourself. For a deeper explanation of what outsourcing involves, see how to outsource Reels editing.

Preparing your footage

Send full sessions as well as selects. A recorded in-store demo or a customer event can produce a handful of Reels from one afternoon.

  • The original clips, vertical if possible
  • Longer recordings that can supply several Reels
  • Example Reels you like
  • Brand fonts, colours and logo
  • Notes on the point of each Reel

Habits that slow a month down

  • Horizontal filming by default
  • A slow opening or a greeting
  • Tiny captions or captions too low on screen
  • A new look every week
  • Forgetting the cover frame

Employee, freelancer or monthly service

Editing in the Instagram app is quick for one clip and weak for anything with captions and sound. An in-house social editor is a good fit for a business with a lot of social work, and costs salary, employer costs and equipment. Freelancers suit campaigns, but each project needs a new brief and the look varies.
