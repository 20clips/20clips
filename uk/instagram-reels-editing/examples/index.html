Hooks to open with

  • Show the finished result in the first second
  • Ask the exact question your customer types into search
  • Start with a surprising close-up
  • Lead with a number or a comparison you can back up
  • Cut to a face reacting before explaining why

Eight formats and ideas

Each one needs ordinary footage, and each can be edited into a finished video for the UK.

Product close-up

A short sequence that shows texture, use and result, cut to music with captions naming the benefit. A UK business can film this on site and send the footage straight to the shared folder.

Day in the life

A fast tour of a working day, with captions and quick cuts that keep it moving. UK founders often have this footage already, because it is what they film on an ordinary working day.

Three tips in thirty seconds

A spoken list, each tip appearing on screen with a punchy caption. This works well for personal brands in the UK who want their expertise to be visible.

Customer reaction

A real reaction to a product or result, edited to start on the reaction. Agencies and consultancies in the UK can use this to show how they work.

Space tour

A walkthrough of a venue, showroom or property, set to music with room names as captions. Independent shops and cafes in the UK can film this during a normal day and edit it later.

Transformation

A start state, a simple process, a finish state. Visual and easy to follow. UK event organisers can adapt this to recap a launch or a talk.

Q and A reply

Answer a real question from your audience in one cut. Many UK accounts use this format to introduce the people behind the brand.

Team or owner introduction

A person introduces who they are and what they do, with captions and light b-roll. In the UK this idea suits retail, services and creator accounts, where personality and product both carry the video.
