Define the job before you search

Hiring a Reels editor starts with two decisions: how many Reels a month, and how much of the look you want to control. A fashion brand with a lookbook calendar and a Manchester venue with weekly events have different requirements. Once the number is clear, the route usually is too.

The UK options are freelancers, an employee or a package service. The cost page shows the numbers for the package, and the service page shows how a month works.

Qualities worth paying for

Framing for the app

A good Reels editor knows where Instagram draws its buttons and keeps your subject and captions out of them.

Strong first frames

The editor should be able to explain how they restructure footage so the Reel opens well.

Controlled caption style

Captions are part of your brand. Ask how fonts and colours are agreed.

A realistic monthly output

Regular posting matters more than occasional brilliance.

Defined revisions

Know how many rounds you get.

Flexible terms

What to ask every candidate

Ask the same questions to each supplier and keep the written answers. UK teams used to working with remote contractors will find a shared-folder system straightforward.

  • How do you frame for the Instagram interface?
  • How do you style and keep captions consistent?
  • How many Reels can you produce each month?
  • How do I send footage and receive feedback?
  • How many revisions does each Reel include?
  • Who looks after trending audio?

What should worry you

  • Reels that look like cropped landscape clips
  • Captions covered by the buttons
  • No revisions allowed
  • Claims about reach or followers

The three routes, side by side

Freelancer

Good for one-off work. A continuous feed means a new brief each time and uneven style.

Employee

Good when Reels are part of a wider social role. It brings salary, employer costs and equipment.

Monthly service

Good for a regular feed: 20 Reels at a fixed fee, with 2 revisions each and nothing locking you in.

Using the first month as a test

Treat the first batch as the real audition. Check that captions are legible on a phone, that the Reels look like your account and that a change request comes back done. If those boxes are ticked, scaling the volume is simple.

It is also a chance to see which of your own footage performs best in the edit. Many UK teams find that behind-the-scenes shots and customer reactions outperform the polished material they expected to lead with, and adjust their filming accordingly.
