How the alternatives compare

Editing in the TikTok app costs nothing and shows. It is fine for a casual clip but poor at branded captions and sound design across twenty videos.

An in-house editor brings salary, employer costs, equipment and management time. That is the right call for a team making video every day, and a lot to carry for about 20 videos a month.

Freelancers charge per job. A single campaign is a good fit; a continuous feed means new briefs and uneven results. The package replaces that with a fixed fee, and the UK hiring guide compares the options.

Practical ways to spend it well

Start with the point. Footage that begins on the interesting moment needs less rescue and holds attention better.

Upload weekly, not monthly. You see what works while there is still time to change direction.

Use long recordings. A livestream replay or a recorded panel can give you many videos from one session.

Outside the package

  • Scripting
  • Filming
  • Posting and scheduling
  • Sounds you license yourself
  • Anything over 20 videos a month

How to judge value after three months

TikTok results come from patterns, not single posts. After a quarter of steady output, compare which hooks held attention, which formats got replies and whether the account looks recognisable. Set that against a fee that never moved.

If the pattern is good, direct more footage at it. If not, the lack of a long-term commitment means you can change or stop.
