Start by defining the account

Before you shortlist editors, write down three things: how many videos you want each month, what voice the account should have and whether you also run paid TikTok. A Manchester bar and a London agency will land on different answers, and the answers shape the route.

What to test for

Retention instincts

Ask how they choose what to cut and what to keep to hold attention.

A feed-native look

Overproduced videos are spotted instantly. Ask to see work that suits the feed.

Caption placement

Captions need to sit where the interface does not cover them.

Audio craft

A sound-on platform exposes weak voice levels and poor music choices.

Dependable monthly volume

Accounts grow on frequency.

A fair commitment

A short term lets you test the fit with your account.

A checklist of questions

Ask each candidate the same questions and compare in writing. A shared-folder process will be familiar to UK teams already using remote contractors.

  • How do you build a hook and a payoff?
  • Where do captions sit in TikTok's interface?
  • How many videos can I expect each month?
  • How do revisions work?
  • Can edits be adapted for Reels and Shorts?
  • Who handles audio licensing?

Danger signs

  • Slick but flat edits
  • A vague answer on audio licensing
  • A single house style for every client
  • Promises about reach

Three routes in short

Freelancer

Right for a campaign. A steady feed means repeated briefing and a style that moves.

Employee

Right when video is a daily task. It adds salary, employer costs and equipment.

Monthly service

Right for a regular feed: 20 videos for $999, a repeatable process and no lock-in.

A note on budgets

The cheapest quote is not always the lowest cost. A low per-video price that adds charges for captions, music and each change can end up above a fixed monthly fee, and a supplier that needs rebriefing every time costs you hours that never show on an invoice. Compare total cost for a month of the volume you actually want.

Using the first batch as an audition

Check the first batch against four tests: how fast the videos open, whether captions clear the interface, whether the audio is clean and whether a change request returns as asked. A provider that passes them is likely to hold the standard.

If a first batch feels generic, say so and send two or three TikToks you would like yours to resemble. Direction is the quickest way to raise quality.
