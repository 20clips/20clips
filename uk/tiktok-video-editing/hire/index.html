What to look for

Six qualities that separate a good TikTok video editing provider from a risky one.

A feel for retention

Ask how the editor decides what to cut and what to keep to hold attention. UK teams that already use remote suppliers will find a shared-folder process familiar.

Native-looking style

TikTok viewers spot overproduced edits. Ask to see work that looks at home in the feed. In the UK it is common to compare several options, so ask each one the same list of questions.

Caption placement

TikTok covers parts of the screen. Captions must sit where they can be read. A strong fit shows in how clearly an editor explains what they need from a UK client.

Sound handling

Clean voice levels and a fitting music bed matter on a sound-on platform. For UK brands posting across several platforms, ask how one set of footage is adapted for each.

Reliable monthly output

TikTok accounts depend on frequency. Check the volume is realistic. Ask how the style of a UK account would be matched, and how that is kept consistent across a month.

Flexible terms

A short commitment lets you test fit with your account. Contract terms matter, and a UK business should not be locked in before seeing the work.

Warning signs that tend to turn into problems later.

No answer on audio licensing

Using unlicensed sounds can cause problems later. UK clients benefit from a clear list of what is not included, so there are no surprises later.

Fixed styles with no input from you

Your brand should shape the look. A UK business should expect to see the process spelled out before any money changes hands.

Growth promises

No editor controls how TikTok distributes a video. Hiring for a company in London or Manchester usually means weighing the speed of setup against the cost over time.
