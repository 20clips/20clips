The brief for a UGC editor is unusual

A UGC editor has to do something slightly contradictory: make footage cleaner and more consistent, while keeping it looking like a real person. Too much polish and it feels like an advert; too little and the audio and pacing lose people. Before you start talking to suppliers, decide how many videos you need and where they will run.

UK brands usually weigh three routes: a freelancer, an in-house editor and a package service. The cost page explains the package fee, and the service page explains the work.

What to look for

Judgement about polish

Ask for examples where footage was cleaned up without losing its voice.

Variation skill

Ask how many different openings can come from one clip.

Product timing

Overlays should arrive on the right words.

Ad-ready delivery

Files should be ready for upload to ad platforms.

Consistency across creators

Different faces, one look.

Transparent scope and terms

Know what is included and how to leave.

Questions to ask

Ask each candidate the same questions and compare written replies. Teams used to working with remote contractors will find a shared-folder method easy.

  • How do you keep UGC authentic?
  • How do you deliver hook variations?
  • How do you time product overlays?
  • How do I send footage from multiple creators?
  • How are revisions handled?
  • What formats do you deliver?

Red flags

  • Over-polished work
  • No hook variations
  • Promises about ad results
  • Vague scope

Three routes in brief

Freelancer

Good for a single campaign. A rolling need means rebriefing and uneven style.

In-house editor

Good for daily creative testing, with salary, employer costs and equipment attached.

Monthly package

Good for steady creator footage: 20 videos for $999, a repeatable process and no lock-in.

Compare on the whole month

A low per-video rate can hide charges for variations, overlays and revisions. Add up a month at the volume you plan and compare totals.

If you brief multiple creators, ask how the supplier keeps a single look across them. That is the harder skill.

The first batch is the audit

Check whether clips still feel like a person, whether the hooks are truly different, whether overlays land on the right words and whether files upload cleanly.

If the first batch misses, send a few ads you admire and explain why.
