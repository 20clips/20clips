What the editor does with a UGC clip

Cleans without sterilising

Audio is balanced, dead air trimmed and the frame corrected, but the voice stays natural. UGC loses its power if it looks staged.

Builds more than one opening

The first seconds decide everything, so the editor builds alternatives from the same clip: a pain point, a result, a question. Variations count within your 20 videos.

Lines up the product

Overlays and shots arrive on the words that mention the product, so claim and proof land together.

Captions for a muted feed

Most paid placements start muted, so the message is carried in text as well as sound.

Exports for use

Vertical files suited to organic posting and to upload to ad platforms.

The monthly workflow

A 30-minute call sets things up. You describe the products, the creators and where the videos run, and agree a delivery schedule.

From there, creator files go into the shared folder as they arrive, with a note of which brief each belongs to. UK teams often upload straight after each creator delivers, which spreads the work across the month and means any style issue is caught early. Videos return for review with 2 revisions each.

Approved videos are final files ready to publish or upload. For the thinking behind this kind of work, read UGC video editing: what brands should look for.

What the editor needs from you

A paragraph of direction is worth a revision round. An editor can polish nearly anything, but they cannot know which claim your compliance team insists on.

  • Original creator files, uncompressed
  • The creative brief and claims that must appear
  • Product images, logos and disclaimers
  • Examples of ads you like
  • A note of the hook or angle to test

Easy ways to waste a revision

  • Over-polishing the footage
  • Testing one opening only
  • Ignoring the muted viewer
  • Cutting the proof out
  • Sending files with no brief

Creators, employees, freelancers or a package

Asking creators to edit their own work produces uneven results. An in-house performance editor is right for a brand testing creative daily, and costs salary, employer costs, equipment and management. A freelancer per video is flexible but needs the same briefing each time.
