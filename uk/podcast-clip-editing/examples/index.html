Hooks to open with

  • Start on the guest's boldest sentence
  • Open on the question
  • Cut to the laugh and back up
  • Lead with the conclusion
  • Use the guest's name and credential on screen

Eight formats and ideas

Each one needs ordinary footage, and each can be edited into a finished video for the UK.

The strongest opinion

A clear stance from a guest, cut to the sentence that states it. Independent shops and cafes in the UK can film this during a normal day and edit it later.

The story

A short anecdote with a beginning and an end. UK event organisers can adapt this to recap a launch or a talk.

The funny exchange

A moment of humour with just enough set-up. Many UK accounts use this format to introduce the people behind the brand.

The lesson

A practical takeaway explained simply. In the UK this idea suits retail, services and creator accounts, where personality and product both carry the video.

The disagreement

A respectful clash of views. A UK business can film this on site and send the footage straight to the shared folder.

The origin story

How a guest started, in under a minute. UK founders often have this footage already, because it is what they film on an ordinary working day.

The quick fire answer

One short answer to a direct question. This works well for personal brands in the UK who want their expertise to be visible.

The teaser

A moment that makes people want the full episode. Agencies and consultancies in the UK can use this to show how they work.

Formats to choose from
