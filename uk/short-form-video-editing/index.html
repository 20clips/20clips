The craft behind a video people finish

An opening that earns the next second

Viewers decide almost immediately. The edit puts the clearest visual or the sharpest line first and removes any warm-up. With a long recording this often means pulling a sentence from the middle to the front.

Rhythm instead of a fixed pace

Cuts, zooms and captions land where the idea changes. Talking-head footage in particular needs breaths and false starts removed without making the speaker sound rushed.

Readable, accurate captions

Many people watch muted on a commute or in a queue, so captions are the message. They are timed to speech, sized for a phone and placed clear of platform buttons.

Sound that was mixed

Voice is levelled, music sits underneath and effects are sparing. A UK brand posting in a crowded feed cannot afford muddy audio.

One look across the month

Fonts, colours and caption style are set from your references on day one and repeated across all 20 videos.

How the monthly process runs

Start with a 30-minute call. You describe what you make, where it is posted and how often footage will arrive, and you agree turnaround and a delivery schedule that fits your volume. There is nothing to install: the work happens in a shared folder.

Drop footage in as it is shot. Many UK clients upload after each shoot day instead of saving everything for month end, which spreads the editing and lets you correct style early. Edited videos return for review, and each includes 2 revisions, so feedback on a caption, a hook or a music choice is part of the process.

Approved videos are finished files. Scheduling and posting stay with you. If you are comparing ways to get this done, this guide to outsourcing editing is a good starting point.

What to send, and what holds a month back

Raw files beat polished ones. A full forty-minute interview lets the editor choose what to use, where a three-minute cut limits them.

  • Original footage straight from the camera or phone
  • Long recordings such as webinars, interviews and talks
  • Videos whose style you like, as references
  • Brand fonts, colours and logo
  • Anything that must be kept in or left out

Common slips

  • Sending only the shortlisted clips, which removes the choice of opening
  • Giving notes from several people in separate emails
  • A different look each week
  • Compressed copies instead of the original files
  • Saving every upload for the last days of the month

Freelancer, in-house or package: the UK view

Hiring a UK editor in-house means salary, employer costs, equipment, software and someone to manage them. It is a good decision for a business with video every day and a poor one for a business that needs around 20 videos a month. Freelancers are flexible, but every project starts with a new brief and the look shifts between people.

A sample month for a UK account

An example of pacing, not a promise about results.

Week one

The call happens, references and brand files go over and the first shoot is uploaded. The first edits arrive and you settle the caption style.

Week two

Send a long recording, such as a customer panel or an in-store session. Approve the first batch and use revisions on videos that are close.

Week three

Film new material shaped by what you liked. Try two different openings on one idea.

Week four

Upload the final batch to use up the 20 videos, then plan next month's shoots. Unused videos do not carry over.

FAQ

What can I send from the UK?

Raw camera footage, phone recordings, podcasts, screen recordings, UGC clips and existing long-form videos are all fine. If you are unsure, raise it on the call.

Do you write the script or the hook?

No. 20CLIPS edits what you provide, including reordering it for a stronger opening. Scripting and filming stay with you.

Will the same video work across platforms?

Usually. The package formats for Reels, TikTok and Shorts together, and small adjustments to captions and pacing cover the differences.

Is VAT or local tax added to the price?

The price is $999 a month in US dollars. For anything about tax treatment, check with your accountant, as the service is delivered remotely.

Can I use it for client work as an agency?

Yes. Agencies commonly use the package for client content. Share each client's references and brand files and the style follows.

How long do edits take?

Turnaround is agreed on your onboarding call, based on volume and how often footage arrives.

Can I swap the style mid-month?

You can, but consistency usually serves an account better. Agree changes through notes during revisions.

Who chooses the music?

Sound design and music are included. If you want a specific track, supply it, because licensing sits with you.

Do unused videos roll over?

No. Spreading footage across the weeks is the easiest way to use all 20.

What if I want to stop?

There is no long-term commitment. You can cancel anytime.
