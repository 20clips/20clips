Decide what you need before you shortlist

Hiring well starts with two answers: how many videos a month, and on which platforms. A London agency producing weekly content for several clients has a different brief from a Manchester restaurant that wants four Reels a week. Once you have a number, the choice between routes becomes much clearer.

UK buyers generally weigh three options. Freelancers are booked per job, an in-house editor joins the payroll, and a package service delivers a set volume for a fixed monthly fee. The cost page shows the arithmetic for the third, and the service page explains what a month looks like.

What separates a good provider from a risky one

They can describe their process

Beautiful work in a portfolio does not show you how a project runs. Ask how footage is shared, who gives feedback and how many revisions are included.

Finishing is part of the price

Accurate captions, music and sound are what make a video usable. If they are extras, the real price is higher than quoted.

They can hold a style

A single standout video proves little. The test is whether twenty look as though they belong together.

They say what is excluded

Good providers are clear that scripting, filming and posting are separate, so you can plan for them.

Cost is predictable

Variable quotes make budgeting hard. A set monthly fee removes the guesswork.

You can leave

A provider confident in its work does not need a long contract to keep you.

A list of questions for any shortlisted supplier

Put the same list to every supplier and compare their written replies. A shared-folder workflow, as used by 20CLIPS, is easy to slot into a UK team that already works with remote contractors.

  • What formats and sizes of file can I send, and how?
  • How many revisions are included in the quote?
  • Are captions, music and sound design included?
  • How will you learn and keep my brand style?
  • What is not covered?
  • Is there a minimum term or exit fee?

Red flags

  • A quote with no list of inclusions
  • A price that changes with every video
  • A long contract before you have seen results
  • Claims that reach or sales are certain
  • Samples that do not look like each other

Side by side, in plain terms

Freelancer

Ideal for a campaign or a one-off format. A regular flow means re-briefing and an inconsistent look.

In-house editor

Ideal when video is a daily task. For a UK employer it adds salary, employer costs and equipment, which is a lot for about 20 videos a month.

Monthly package

Ideal for steady short-form content. $999 for 20 videos, a fixed process, 2 revisions each and no long-term commitment.

FAQ

Freelancer or package for UK short-form video?

A package suits a steady flow because the process and price repeat. A freelancer suits one campaign.

When does an in-house editor make sense?

When video work is constant and well beyond about 20 videos a month.

What do I need to get started?

Raw footage, brand fonts and colours and a few reference videos in a shared folder.

Can the editor match my current look?

Yes. References and guidelines are gathered at onboarding.

How can I test the fit first?

Start with one month. There is no long-term commitment.

Do you post for me?

No. You or your team publish the finished files.

What if I am not happy with a video?

Each includes 2 revisions, so send clear notes.

Do you work with agencies?

Yes. Many agencies use the package for client content.
