How Much Does Short-Form Video Editing Cost?

20CLIPS Blog · 6 min read

Short-form video editing pricing varies more than most people expect, mostly because "editing a video" can mean very different amounts of work. A single caption pass on a clean talking-head clip is a different job from cutting 20 hook variations out of raw UGC. Here's how the main pricing models actually break down.

Freelance editors

Freelance rates typically fall into two structures: per-video and hourly. Per-video rates for short-form content commonly range from around $15 to $50 for entry-level editors, and $50 to $150 or more for experienced editors who understand hooks, pacing and platform formatting. Hourly rates generally run $20 to $75 per hour, and a single short-form video can take anywhere from 30 minutes to a few hours depending on complexity.

The upside of freelancers is flexibility. The downside is variability: quality, availability and turnaround time can all shift from month to month, and managing multiple freelancers to hit a consistent volume adds real coordination overhead.

In-house editors

Hiring a full-time short-form editor typically means a salary in the $40,000 to $70,000+ range depending on experience and location, plus software, equipment and management time. This can make sense at high volume, but it's a fixed cost that doesn't flex if your content needs change month to month.

Agencies and subscription services

Editing agencies and subscription-style services usually price around a set volume of videos per month rather than an hourly or per-video rate. Monthly packages commonly range from $500 to $3,000+ depending on the number of videos, revision policy and turnaround time included. A flat monthly model: for example, 20 videos a month for $999: works out to roughly $50 per video, but the real value is the predictability: one price, one workflow, no invoices per video.

What actually drives the price

  • Volume: how many videos you need per month
  • Turnaround time: faster delivery generally costs more
  • Revisions: unlimited or high-revision policies cost more to sustain
  • Complexity: motion graphics, multiple hook variations and multi-platform formatting all add editing time
  • Sourcing: raw UGC and podcast footage usually take longer to edit than clean, pre-shot content

The bottom line

If you need a handful of videos occasionally, a freelancer on a per-video basis is usually the simplest option. If you need consistent monthly volume across Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, a flat-rate monthly service tends to be the more predictable and cost-efficient option, since the price doesn't change as your footage volume grows within the plan.
