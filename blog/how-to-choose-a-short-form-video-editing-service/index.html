How to Choose a Short-Form Video Editing Service

20CLIPS Blog · 6 min read

Short-form video editing services vary widely in quality, pricing model and process. Here's a practical checklist for evaluating one before you commit.

1. Look at the portfolio, not just the pitch

Ask to see real examples relevant to your content type: talking-head, podcast, UGC, or product footage. A service that only shows highly produced brand content may not be the right fit if your footage is raw and unscripted, or vice versa.

2. Understand the pricing model

Pricing generally falls into three models: hourly, per-video, or flat monthly volume. Flat monthly pricing: a fixed number of videos for a fixed price: tends to be easiest to budget for and scale with, since cost doesn't shift as your footage volume grows within the plan.

3. Check turnaround time

Ask directly how long it typically takes to get videos back after sending footage. This affects how far in advance you need to plan and how responsive your content calendar can be.

4. Clarify the revision policy

Understand how many rounds of revisions are included and what counts as a revision versus a new request. Unclear or unlimited-sounding revision policies can be a red flag as often as they're a benefit.

5. Confirm platform coverage

Make sure the service actually formats for the platforms you need: Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts each have slightly different conventions, even if the underlying footage is the same.

6. Ask about your specific content type

If most of your content comes from a podcast, UGC, or long-form recordings, confirm the service has real experience turning that specific type of raw material into short-form video, not just editing pre-shot, clean footage.

7. Look for a simple, repeatable process

The best services run on a clear loop: you send footage, they edit, you review, you receive finished videos: on a predictable monthly cadence. If the process sounds complicated to manage, it will be complicated to manage.

8. Confirm there's no long-term lock-in

A service confident in its work shouldn't need to lock you into a long contract. Month-to-month terms are a reasonable standard to expect.
