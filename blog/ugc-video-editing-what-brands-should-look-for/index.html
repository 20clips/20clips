UGC Video Editing: What Brands Should Look For

20CLIPS Blog · 5 min read

Raw UGC footage is only as good as the edit behind it. Two brands can get the same quality of raw creator footage and end up with very different results depending on how it's edited. Here's what to actually look for when choosing a UGC editing service or editor.

Hook variation ability

Strong UGC editing doesn't produce one version of a video: it produces several, each with a different opening line or angle, so you can test what resonates. If a service only delivers a single cut per piece of footage, you lose most of the value of having UGC in the first place.

Retention-focused pacing

Look for editors who actively cut for pacing: removing dead air, tightening pauses, cutting to the point: rather than lightly trimming raw footage and calling it done. Retention is the difference between UGC that performs and UGC that doesn't.

Clean captions and on-brand overlays

Captions should be accurate, well-timed and legible, and any on-screen product overlays or callouts should match your brand's visual style rather than looking like a generic template slapped on top.

Ability to serve both organic and paid use cases

The best UGC editing works for both your organic feed and your ad account, since the underlying footage often serves both. An editor who understands both contexts: a scroll-stopping organic post and a conversion-focused ad creative: gets more value out of the same footage.

Realistic turnaround and revision process

UGC campaigns often move fast, especially around launches or promotions. Ask about typical turnaround time and how revisions are handled before committing, so there are no surprises when you need creative quickly.

Red flags to watch for

  • No captions included by default
  • Only one version delivered per piece of raw footage
  • Generic templates applied regardless of your brand
  • Unclear or per-request pricing with no flat monthly option
