What makes a clip worth publishing

Five things the editor looks after.

A moment that stands alone

The best clips are a point, a story or a disagreement that makes sense without the episode around it. Finding them means listening to the whole recording, not skimming a transcript.

A tight cut of real conversation

Speech has false starts, tangents and long pauses that work in an hour but not in forty seconds. Tightening them while keeping the rhythm of the speaker is the skill that separates a clip from an excerpt.

Two voices in a vertical frame

Podcast video is often two or three people at microphones in a wide shot. In a vertical clip the editor cuts between speakers and keeps the active one in view, so it feels like a conversation, not a crop.

Captions that follow the talk

Spoken clips depend on captions that are accurate, timed to speech, easy to read and styled to the show.

A branded look across the show

A recognisable caption style and palette make clips from the same show feel connected. Your references set it for all 20 videos.

How a month of clips runs

A 30-minute call comes first. You describe the show, how many episodes you record and what you want clips to do for it. The delivery schedule is agreed there.

Then you share the show's logo, fonts, colours and a few clips you like, and upload each episode to a shared folder as it is recorded. Video recordings give the strongest clips, though audio-only shows can use simple visuals and strong captions. Finished clips return for review with 2 revisions each, and you can flag timestamps you want included.

Approved clips are final files. Posting stays with you. For the method in more depth, read how to turn a podcast into short-form content.

What to send with each episode

Record video even if the show is audio-first. Faces make the clips far more engaging, and a camera on each speaker in a Dubai studio costs very little against the extra reach.

  • The full episode recording, with video where possible
  • Guest names so captions are spelled correctly
  • Your show logo, fonts and colours
  • Timestamps for moments you already like
  • Clips from other shows whose style you admire

Common slips

  • Sending only audio when video exists
  • Clipping a punchline without its set-up
  • Making clips as long as the topic
  • Leaving the guest's name out
  • Posting clips only when an episode drops

Do it yourself, hire a producer or subscribe

Clipping one or two moments yourself is realistic. Twenty a month on top of recording and publishing the show is not. A producer on the payroll suits a network with a full production team, and in the UAE means salary, visa and equipment. A freelancer per episode is flexible, though each episode needs a new brief and the look can drift.
