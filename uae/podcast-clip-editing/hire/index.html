Clip editing needs a good ear

Podcast clips are not simply trimmed excerpts. The editor has to hear which moment stands alone, cut the speech so it flows and frame two or three speakers in a vertical picture. Before you hire, decide how many clips you want each month and whether you record video.

UAE shows usually weigh a freelancer, an in-house producer and a package service. For the money side see the cost page, and for the practical side see the service page.

Six things to test

A good ear for moments

Ask how the editor chooses clips from a full recording.

Speaker framing

Cutting between speakers in a vertical frame is a skill.

Accurate captions

Spoken clips depend on captions that follow the talk.

Consistent show branding

Clips should look like they belong to the same show.

Reliable volume

A steady stream of clips keeps episodes visible.

Flexible terms

Test the fit before a long commitment.

Questions to ask

Ask every candidate the same questions and compare in writing. A shared-folder method suits UAE teams used to remote suppliers.

  • How do you pick clips?
  • How do you frame several speakers?
  • How are captions styled?
  • How many clips per month?
  • How are revisions handled?
  • Can I choose timestamps?

Warning signs

  • Clips with no context
  • Long, rambling clips
  • Misspelled names
  • Promises about listeners

The three routes

Freelancer

Good for a single episode or a test. A weekly show means a new brief each time.

In-house producer

Good for a full production team. In the UAE it adds salary, visa and equipment.

Monthly package

Good for a weekly show: 20 clips for $999, a repeatable process and no lock-in.

Compare total monthly cost

A low per-clip price can hide charges for captions, framing and revisions. Add up a month at the volume you plan and compare totals.

If you record several episodes in a busy week, ask how each supplier copes. A fixed monthly volume spread across the weeks tends to be simpler than paying per clip.

Make the first batch your audition

Check that every clip makes sense alone, that names are spelled correctly, that the framing keeps the right speaker in view and that the style looks like your show.

If something is off, send two or three clips from shows you admire and say what you like.
