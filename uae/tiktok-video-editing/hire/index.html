A TikTok editor needs a feel for the platform

TikTok edits are not just trimmed clips. They are built around retention, native style and sound, and an editor who does well on Instagram may not automatically understand TikTok. Before you hire, decide how many videos you want and what sort of voice the account should have.

UAE businesses usually choose between a freelancer, an in-house editor and a package. The cost page explains the package price, and the service page explains what each month contains.

Six things to test

A feel for retention

Ask how the editor decides what to cut and what to keep to hold attention.

Native-looking style

Viewers spot overproduced edits quickly. Ask to see work that looks at home in the feed.

Caption placement

TikTok covers parts of the screen, so captions have to sit where they can be read.

Sound handling

Clean voice levels and a fitting music bed matter on a sound-on platform.

Reliable monthly output

Accounts grow on frequency, so check the volume is realistic.

Flexible terms

A short commitment lets you test fit with your account.

Questions to put to each candidate

Compare written answers rather than pitches. A shared-folder workflow suits UAE teams already used to working with remote suppliers.

  • How do you structure a hook and a payoff?
  • How are captions placed for TikTok's interface?
  • How many videos do I get each month?
  • How are revisions handled?
  • Can the same edit be adapted for Reels and Shorts?
  • Who is responsible for audio licensing?

Warning signs

  • Polished but lifeless edits
  • No answer on audio licensing
  • One fixed style for every client
  • Promises about growth

The routes at a glance

Freelancer

Good for a single campaign. A regular feed means repeated briefing and variable quality.

In-house editor

Good when video is a daily job. It adds salary, visa and equipment.

Monthly package

Good for steady output: 20 videos for $999, a repeatable process and no lock-in.

Why the first batch is the real test

Judge any TikTok editor on the first batch you receive. Do the videos open fast, do the captions sit clear of the interface, does the audio sound clean and does a request for a different hook come back as asked? A provider who passes those four checks will probably hold the standard across the month.

In the UAE, where feeds are crowded and audiences compare you with the next video, the first second matters even more. If a first batch feels generic, say so in your notes and share two or three TikToks you would be proud to resemble.
