What good short-form editing looks like

Five things you should be able to see in every finished video.

The first second carries the point

The opening shows the result, the surprising line or the question. Greetings and logo animations are cut. When footage is a long recording, the editor often moves the best sentence to the front.

Pacing follows the speech

Dead air goes, and the picture changes when the idea changes. A zoom, a cutaway or a caption highlight lands on the word that matters instead of on a fixed timer.

Captions are part of the design

They are accurate, large enough for a phone held at arm's length, timed to the speaker and kept clear of the buttons each platform draws on top of the video.

Sound is finished, not left raw

Voice levels are balanced, music sits under the speech and effects are used sparingly. Cheap audio loses a viewer faster than a plain picture.

The look stays consistent

Fonts, colours, caption style and transitions are agreed at onboarding from your references, then repeated across all 20 videos so the feed looks like one account.

How a month works, step by step

Everything starts with a 30-minute call. You describe what you film and where you publish, share a few reference videos and brand files, and agree how often footage will arrive. Turnaround and your delivery schedule are set on that call, based on your volume, rather than promised in advance.

After that, footage goes into a shared folder whenever you have it. Most UAE clients send in batches: a shoot day in Dubai Marina on Tuesday, a recorded podcast on Thursday, a launch event at the weekend. Each batch is edited into videos that come back for review. Every video includes 2 revisions, so a changed caption or a different opening is a note, not a new order.

Approved videos are finished files. Posting stays with you or your social team, as does anything that requires a decision about timing, so there is nothing to learn and no software to install. This explainer describes what an editor actually does in each step.

What to send, and what to avoid sending

The most useful habit is to send full recordings along with clips. A single long conversation recorded in a Downtown studio can supply several of your 20 videos, because the editor picks the self-contained moments instead of working with whatever you pre-selected.

  • Raw camera or phone footage in the best quality you have
  • Longer recordings, such as podcasts, webinars and talks, that hold several short moments
  • Reference videos and your brand fonts, colours and logo
  • Timestamps for any line that must be included
  • Licensed music only if you want a specific track

Mistakes that cost a revision

  • Footage already compressed by a social app, which lowers quality
  • Only the highlights, leaving no choice of opening
  • Changing the brand look every week
  • Briefing five people separately instead of sending combined notes
  • Waiting until the last week of the month to send everything

Freelancer, in-house editor or a monthly package

UAE companies usually weigh three routes. A freelancer is flexible, but briefing starts again with every job and quality depends on who is free that week. An in-house editor gives you someone embedded in the business, with visa, salary, equipment and management time attached, which pays off only when there is constant video work well beyond 20 a month.

A monthly package sits between them. The price and the process stay the same each month, the style is matched to your references, and you can cancel anytime because there is no long-term commitment. A sample month for a UAE account

An illustration of the rhythm, not a forecast of views or followers.

Week one

Book the call, share references and send the first shoot: a showroom, a menu launch or a founder recording. Review the first edits and settle the caption style.

Week two

Send a longer recording with several moments in it. Approve the first batch and use revisions on anything close but not final.

Week three

Film around what the first batch showed you worked, then upload it. Ask for a different opening on one video to compare.

Week four

Use up the remaining videos with a final batch and start planning next month's shoots. Unused videos do not roll over, so batches are best spread across the weeks.

FAQ

What counts as footage I can send from the UAE?

Raw camera footage, phone recordings, podcast recordings, screen recordings, UGC clips and existing long-form videos all work. If you are unsure whether something will cut well, ask on the 30-minute call.

Do you write scripts or hooks from scratch?

No. 20CLIPS edits and optimises the footage you provide, which includes trimming and reordering it so the strongest moment opens the video. It does not write scripts or film new material.

Can the same video go on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube?

Often yes. The package covers Reels, TikTok and Shorts formatting, and small changes to captions and pacing let one edit serve more than one platform.

Is the price quoted in US dollars?

Yes. The price is $999 a month, in US dollars, for 20 videos, with no per-video fees and no long-term commitment.

Do unused videos roll over?

No. Most clients send footage in batches and use all 20 each month once the workflow is set up.

Through the shared folder. Raw recordings are welcome, and longer ones often give the best choice of moments.

Can I ask for a different style?

Yes. Share reference videos and brand guidelines during onboarding and the style is matched across all 20 videos.

How quickly do I get videos back?

Turnaround is set on your onboarding call, based on your monthly volume and how often you send footage.

Who handles music?

Sound design and music are part of every video. If you want a specific track, you supply it, because licensing sits with you.
