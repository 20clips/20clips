How the monthly cycle runs

It begins with a 30-minute call. You describe what you make, share reference videos and brand files and agree how often footage will arrive. Turnaround and your delivery schedule are agreed there, based on volume.

Each month then follows the same cycle. Footage goes into a shared folder, edited videos come back for review and you use your 2 revisions per video. Approved videos are finished files for you to publish. Most UAE clients upload in batches, for example a shoot on Tuesday and a recorded session on Friday, and use all 20 videos across the weeks.

Unused videos do not roll over, so planning footage across the month is the key habit. For help with volume, read how many short-form videos to post each week.

What the package does not do

The package edits footage you provide. That clarity is part of what keeps the price flat: scope is fixed, so the fee is too.

If you need more volume, raise it on the 30-minute call and it is discussed before you commit.

Good to know up front

  • It does not write scripts or plan content
  • It does not film new footage
  • It does not post or schedule
  • It does not include licensed music of your own choice
  • The core offer is 20 videos, not more

How the package compares with the alternatives

Editing in-house gives you someone embedded in the business, with a salary, visa, equipment and management time. It makes sense when you have constant video work well beyond 20 videos a month. A freelancer is flexible, but you brief again for each job and the output depends on who is free that week. An agency retainer often includes strategy and filming as well as editing, which may be what you need but costs more than editing alone.

The package is the middle option: a fixed price, a repeatable process and no lock-in. For the figures see what a monthly editing package costs in the UAE, and for a checklist see how to choose a monthly editing package in the UAE.
