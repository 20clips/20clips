What a good Short does differently

Five parts of the craft.

It stands alone

A good Short is one idea that makes sense without the rest of the video. Context a viewer needs is pulled forward, and anything they do not need is dropped.

The hook is written by the edit

In the feed a Short competes with the next one a swipe away. The opening shows the result, the surprising line or the question, and the set-up follows.

The frame is rebuilt for vertical

Long-form footage is almost always 16:9. The edit reframes around the speaker or the action so the subject fills the screen, and does not leave a tiny person in the middle of empty bars.

Captions and graphics carry the point

Accurate captions, a few emphasised words and simple graphics make a Short readable at a glance and with the sound off.

It points back to your channel

A Short works hardest when it makes people curious about the full video. Whether Shorts feed your long-form views or stand alone is a choice you make about the channel, and posting and linking stay with you.

How a month of Shorts runs

It starts with a 30-minute call about the channel, the long-form videos you already have and what you want Shorts to do for it. Turnaround and your delivery schedule are agreed there.

You then share long-form files, a few Shorts whose style you like and your brand fonts and colours. If you know your strongest moments, send timestamps. If not, the editor finds candidates from the recording. Finished Shorts return for review with 2 revisions on each, so a different opening or a trimmed ending is quick to ask for.

Approved Shorts are ready to upload to your channel. For ideas on volume, read how many short-form videos to post each week.

What to send, and what to leave out

Send the whole recording, not a pre-cut highlight. A full session in a Dubai studio or a conference hall offers far more choice than a three-minute teaser.

  • The full-length source video or recording in the best quality you have
  • Timestamps of moments you already like
  • Existing Shorts you want the new ones to resemble
  • Your channel's fonts, colours and logo
  • Anything that must not be cut, such as a key claim

Slips to avoid

  • Cutting a clip without its set-up
  • Leaving a slow opening in
  • Keeping the wide framing
  • Treating Shorts as leftovers
  • Posting once and stopping
