Turning long videos into Shorts is a specific skill

Cutting a good Short from a long recording is not the same as trimming. The editor has to find a moment that stands on its own, rebuild the opening, reframe a wide picture for vertical and caption it all. Before you hire, decide how many Shorts you want and what sort of long-form material you have.

UAE channels usually choose between a freelancer, an in-house editor and a package. The cost page explains the package price, and the service page explains what a month contains.

Six things to check

Choosing self-contained moments

Ask how the editor finds moments that make sense without the rest of the video.

Reframing skill

A wide shot must be reframed around the speaker, not simply cropped.

Strong openings

The first seconds decide a Short. Ask how hooks are built.

Captions that follow speech

Accurate, timed captions are essential.

Volume and rhythm

Shorts reward regular posting, so check the monthly volume.

Flexible terms

Test the fit before committing for long.

Questions for each candidate

Ask the same questions of each supplier and compare their written answers. UAE teams used to remote suppliers will find a shared-folder workflow familiar.

  • How do you pick moments from a long video?
  • How do you reframe a horizontal source?
  • How are captions styled?
  • How many Shorts per month are included?
  • How are revisions handled?
  • Can you adapt the same edit for Reels and TikTok?

Warning signs

  • Cuts that make no sense without context
  • Letterboxed videos in a vertical frame
  • No captions
  • Promises about subscribers

How the routes compare

Freelancer

Good for a test batch. A regular channel means repeated briefing and a drifting style.

In-house editor

Good when video is a daily job. In the UAE it adds salary, visa and equipment.

Monthly package

Good for a steady rhythm: 20 Shorts for $999, 2 revisions each and no lock-in.

Think in total cost, not per-clip price

A low quote per Short can hide extra charges for captions, reframing or revisions, and a supplier you must rebrief each time costs hours that never appear on an invoice. Add up the total for the month of the volume you actually want and compare that number across the options.

For a UAE channel the question is also whether the supplier can keep up when you record a lot in one week, such as after a conference. A fixed monthly volume with batches spread across the weeks tends to cope better than a pay-per-clip arrangement.

Why the first batch tells you most

Check four things on the first batch: whether each Short makes sense alone, whether the opening is strong, whether the reframing looks natural and whether captions are accurate. A provider who gets those right will probably keep the standard.

If a first batch feels weak, share two or three Shorts you admire and say what you liked. Direction raises quality faster than anything else.
