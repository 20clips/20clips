A room has to be felt in a few seconds

Interior design is visual by nature, and short video lets a space be experienced in seconds. Reveals, walkthroughs and detail shots need slow, confident pacing, clean colour and just enough text to name the room or the style. Over-editing makes a calm space feel busy.

Common mistakes in design content

Over-editing a calm space

Fast cuts and heavy effects make a serene room feel busy. Slow, smooth movement suits the subject.

Shooting in mixed light

Daylight and warm bulbs together confuse colour. Choose one and turn the others off.

Forgetting the details

Textures, finishes and objects are what people remember. Film them as carefully as the full room.

Shaky movement

A gimbal or a steady surface makes a room look expensive. Handheld shake makes it look rushed.

How a design reel comes together

The editor opens on the full room or the most beautiful surface, then moves through the details in a smooth sequence. Colour is kept honest and consistent so materials look as they do in person, and sound is gentle, usually a soft music bed with no sharp effects.

Captions, if used at all, name the room, the material or the concept. Because the pace is slow, a single walkthrough can be cut into a reveal, a detail reel and a before-and-after, which spreads one visit across several posts.
