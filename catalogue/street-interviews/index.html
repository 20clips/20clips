One question, many short answers

Street interviews, or vox pops, work because they are honest and fast: a question, a few quick answers and a punchline. The edit finds the best responses, cuts between them quickly and captions everything so it works with the sound off, which matters when street audio is noisy.

The two examples are a beach interview with a branded microphone and a two-option question on screen, and an interview in front of a branded backdrop with a playful question about music. Both are cheap to film and easy to repeat.

Who this is for

Brands, events, venues and creators use street interviews to get real voices. See TikTok in the UAE, in the UK and in Australia for how 20 videos a month fit a repeating format.

What makes street interviews work

Ask one question

A single, clear question keeps the video focused.

Cut the best answers together

The edit keeps the funniest, most honest and most surprising replies.

Caption everything

Street audio is noisy, so captions do the work.

End on a punchline

The last answer should land.

What to send

Ask the same question of ten people and keep every take. The editor can only choose the best answers if you filmed enough of them.
