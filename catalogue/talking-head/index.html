The most common format and the one that needs editing most

A person talking to the camera is the most common format in short-form video and the one that most needs editing. Cutting every dead breath, adding captions and emphasis and using cutaways turn a recording into a video people watch to the end.

The example below is a host seated at a microphone in front of purple lighting, framed and captioned for a vertical screen. It sits in the podcast niche too, and it shows the same principles that apply to any founder, coach or expert speaking to camera.

Who this is for

What makes talking-head edits work

Cut the dead air

Every pause that does not help the point is removed.

Add emphasis

Highlighted words and small zooms keep attention.

Use cutaways

Supporting visuals break up a single shot.

Keep one idea

A single point per video is easier to follow.

What to send

Record in a quiet room and put the microphone close. Audio quality is the biggest single difference between a polished clip and a rough one.

  • Raw recordings
  • A note of the point you want to make
  • Brand fonts and colours
  • Reference videos
  • Any lines that must stay in
