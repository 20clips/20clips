Automotive short-form editing

Cars are made for the camera, but raw footage of a vehicle rarely works as short-form video on its own. The shine, the sound and the details have to be paced, framed and finished for a vertical screen. Showroom features, product walk-arounds and event reels all benefit from tight cutting, clean audio and captions.

What makes automotive edits work

Open on the strongest detail

The best automotive hook is usually a close-up: a badge, a headlight, an engine note. The edit moves it to the start.

Let the sound do work

Engine and ambient sound matter as much as picture, so sound design is part of every video.

Frame for vertical

Long, low vehicles need careful reframing so they fill a 9:16 screen.

Keep captions short

Specs and names on screen should be quick to read and sit clear of the interface.

What to send

  • Raw walk-around and detail footage
  • Interior and exterior shots
  • Audio of the engine or the room
  • Spec points you want to mention
  • Reference videos from brands you admire
