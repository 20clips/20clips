Fashion short-form is product, personality and pace

A menswear feature, a campaign reel or a creator wearing the product all need edits that show fit, texture and movement, and that keep text out of the way of the garment. The edit chooses how much to show, how fast to cut and where the brand mark sits.

The two examples here pull in opposite directions. One is a bright outdoor lifestyle reel of a man walking in daylight with the brand mark in frame, edited to keep the movement of the clothes central. The other is a quiet studio feature on a plain grey backdrop with minimal text and slow pacing.

Who this is for

Labels, boutiques, stylists and creators film fittings and lookbooks all year. The package covers UGC editing in the UAE, Reels in the UK and TikTok in Australia, for 20 videos a month at a flat $999.

What makes fashion edits work

Show the product in motion

Clothes look best when they move. The edit uses motion, not stills.

Keep captions light

Text should name the product without hiding it.

Vary the hook

Different openings from the same footage show which angle works.

Match the tone to the brand

A relaxed daylight reel and a studio feature call for different pacing, and the edit follows the brand.

What to send

Shoot the same look in daylight and in the studio if you can. Contrast between the two makes a month of content feel varied.
