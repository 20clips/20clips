A podcast clip has to stand on its own

A podcast episode holds many short moments, and clips are how people discover a show. A good clip starts on the strongest line, makes sense without the episode around it and uses captions that follow the speech. The edit chooses, tightens and frames each moment for a vertical screen.

The two examples are an interview clip, where a guest sits at a microphone with a bold caption that sets up the story, and a highlight where a host sits against purple studio lighting, framed and captioned for portrait. Between them they cover the two layouts most shows need.

What makes podcast clips work

Common mistakes in podcast clips

Clipping the punchline without its set-up

A line that was funny in context can fall flat alone. A few seconds of build-up fix it.

Making clips as long as the topic

A clip should hold one idea. Anything longer should be a second clip.

Leaving off the guest's name

A caption with the guest's name and role makes a clip feel credible and easier to share.

Posting only on release day

Spreading clips across the weeks keeps an episode in front of people for longer.

How a podcast clip is made

The editor listens through the recording for moments that stand alone, then tightens the speech by removing false starts and long pauses without flattening the speaker's rhythm. In a vertical frame the picture cuts between speakers so whoever is talking is in view, and captions follow every word.

A branded caption style and colour palette are applied so clips from different episodes look related. The finished clip is formatted for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, and you can flag timestamps for any moment you want included.
