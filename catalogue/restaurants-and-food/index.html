Food and drink are made for short video

A pour, a sizzle, a table shot or a venue tour tells a story in seconds, but only if it is edited well: quick cuts, strong audio and captions that name the dish or the place. Poor sound and a slow opening lose a viewer before the food arrives.

The five edits below cover most of what a restaurant publishes: a venue film that opens on the words 'Pure Veg Dining', a vintage-bar feature that opens on a plate of pasta, a travel and food feature that starts at a landmark, a bar promo built from a slow cocktail stir and a glossy close-up of a cocktail shaker.

What makes food edits work

The pour, the steam or the plating makes the strongest first second.

Common mistakes in food content

Filming only the finished plate

The pour, the steam, the knife and the pass are what make food look alive.

Shooting in unflattering light

Overhead strip lights flatten a dish. Side light from a window works better.

Leaving out the sound

Sizzle, ice and chatter are half the appeal. Record them.

Forgetting the name of the dish

A short caption helps people remember what to order.

How a food video is cut

The editor opens on the most appetising shot, such as a plate with a bold title or a slow cocktail stir, and then builds quickly through close-ups, the venue and the people. Sound design brings up the sizzle and the ice, music keeps the tempo, and captions name the dish or the offer.

Venue films and drinks promos suit a slower, more composed pace, while a food and travel feature can move between locations and a presenter. One service can supply several videos: a venue film, a signature dish, a drinks promo and a chef feature.
