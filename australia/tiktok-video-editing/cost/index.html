TikTok editing with 20CLIPS costs $999 a month in US dollars for 20 videos, or about $50 each. The fee covers hook and pacing work, captions and subtitles, b-roll, sound design and music, basic motion graphics, 9:16 delivery and 2 revisions per video.

Owners and managers in Australia can plan on a single figure. There is no per-video price and no setup fee, and captions and music are not extras. For the work itself see the service page, and for the full list see the pricing page.

What influences a TikTok editing bill

How often you post

TikTok rewards frequent posting, and the bill follows. Twenty a month is about five a week.

The hook work

Retention-focused editing means trimming and reordering every clip. That is part of the package.

Captions and sound

Readable captions and clean audio take time. They are included.

The footage itself

Clean audio and steady framing speed things up. Noisy or shaky clips take more care.

Revision rounds

Two are included per video, which covers most changes.

The alternatives, side by side

Editing in the app is free and slow. It does a casual clip well and a branded month of twenty videos badly.

Employing someone brings wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision. That suits a team making video every day and is heavy for about 20 videos a month.

Freelancers charge by the job. That suits a campaign. For a steady feed you re-brief every time and accept some drift. The package is a fixed fee with a repeatable process, and the Australian hiring guide sets out the trade-offs.

Getting more from the fee

Open on the point. Footage that starts at the interesting moment needs less cutting and holds attention better.

Upload in weekly batches. You see what works early enough to change direction.

Use long recordings. A recorded class or a livestream can supply many videos.

Not in the price

  • Scripts
  • Filming
  • Posting and scheduling
  • Sounds you license yourself
  • Beyond 20 videos a month

Is it worth it for a small business?

Think about what an hour of your time is worth, then count the hours twenty edited videos would take you. Most owner-operators find the figure is a good deal higher than they expected, and that the real cost of editing yourself is the work you did not do while you were doing it.

If your audience is small and local, a lighter plan may suit better, and the 30-minute call is the place to talk about it before you commit.
