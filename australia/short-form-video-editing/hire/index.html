How to hire short-form video editing in Australia: freelancer, in-house or monthly package

How to hire short-form video editing in Australia: what to look for in a provider, the questions to ask, and how freelancers, employees and a monthly package compare.

First, size the job

Before you contact anyone, work out how many videos you need each month and where they will go. A Melbourne cafe filming daily service and a Brisbane coach recording one weekly session need very different things. The number often answers the question of whether to hire someone, use a freelancer or subscribe to a package.

Australian businesses typically choose between three routes: a freelancer paid per project, an employee, or a package service that delivers a set monthly volume. The cost guide gives the figures for the third, and the service page shows how a month runs.

Signs of a good editing partner

A clear way of working

A strong reel shows taste, not logistics. Ask how files are shared, how feedback works and how many revisions are included.

Finishing included

Captions, music and sound design make a video publishable. If they cost extra, your real bill is higher than the quote.

Consistent output

One great video is easy. Twenty that match each other takes a process.

Upfront about limits

A trustworthy supplier lists what they do not do, such as scripting, filming and posting.

Predictable pricing

A flat monthly fee is easier to run a business on than a quote for every job.

A fair exit

Short or no minimum terms mean the supplier has to keep earning your business.

Ask before signing anything

Send identical questions to every candidate and compare the written responses. If you already work with suppliers in other cities, a shared-folder workflow like the one 20CLIPS uses will feel familiar.

  • How do I send footage and in what formats?
  • How many revisions are included?
  • Are captions and music part of the price?
  • How will you learn my brand style?
  • What is not included?
  • What is the minimum term and how do I cancel?

Things to watch for

  • No list of inclusions
  • Pricing that varies per video
  • Long contracts up front
  • Promises of followers or sales
  • Sample work in wildly different styles

The three routes in a nutshell

Freelancer

Good for a single campaign or a specialised format. For a regular flow you re-brief each time and the look drifts.

Employee

Good when video is a daily job. In Australia the cost includes wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision, which is heavy for about 20 videos a month.

Monthly package

Good for a steady supply of short-form content: a fixed process and 20 videos for $999, with 2 revisions on each and nothing locking you in.

FAQ

Package, freelancer or employee in Australia?

A package suits steady content with a fixed price. A freelancer suits one-off work, and an employee suits constant video work.

When is an employee worth it?

When video is a daily job and the volume goes well beyond about 20 videos a month.

What do I send to start?

Raw footage, brand fonts and colours and a few reference videos, in a shared folder.

Can the editor match my current style?

Yes. References and brand guidelines are collected at onboarding.

How can I test the service?

Start with a single month, as there is no long-term commitment.

Do you post for me?

No. Posting is for you or your team.

What if a video is off?

Each has 2 revisions, so send clear notes.
