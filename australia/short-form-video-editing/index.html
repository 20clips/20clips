Why a remote editor suits the way Australians work

Australia is a big country and a lot of businesses run across it: a coach in Brisbane serving clients in Perth, a trades business with crews in several suburbs, an online store whose owner films in one city while the stock sits in another. Video suits that spread, because a phone clip says more than a paragraph, but someone still has to edit it.

20CLIPS is a remote service, so the geography does not matter. You film, upload the raw footage to a shared folder and send a few references. An editor turns it into 9:16 videos with a strong opening, captions, b-roll, sound and motion graphics, formatted for Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. What a well-edited short video has

Check any finished video against these.

A first second that gives a reason to stay

The strongest line or image goes first. Greetings and slow fades are removed, and with long recordings the best moment is often moved to the front.

Cuts that follow the idea

The picture changes when the thought changes. Pauses are removed without making the speaker sound hurried.

Captions that carry the video

A lot of people watch without sound. Captions are accurate, easy to read at arm's length and kept away from the on-screen buttons.

Audio that was tidied

Voice is levelled, music sits under the speech and effects are used with restraint. Wind on a job site and echo in a hall are common problems that a good edit manages.

Brand consistency

Fonts, colours and caption style come from your references and repeat across all 20 videos.

How a month runs

It starts with a 30-minute call to cover what you film, where you publish and how often you will send footage. Turnaround and your delivery schedule are agreed there, based on your volume, so nobody is working to an assumption.

Then you upload footage as and when it exists. Many Australian clients send a batch after each shoot instead of waiting for the month to end. Each video comes back for review with 2 revisions included, which means a different opening or a changed caption is a note rather than a new request.

When a video is approved it is a finished file. Posting stays with you or your team, so you can schedule it with the rest of your content. For a plain-language description of the role, see what a short-form video editor does.

Sending footage well

If you can, film vertically and record clean sound. A cheap clip-on microphone helps more than a better camera, because audio problems are the hardest to hide.

  • Original footage from the camera or phone
  • Longer recordings of talks, sessions and interviews
  • A few videos you want yours to feel like
  • Brand fonts, colours and logo
  • Notes on lines or moments that must stay

Easy ways to lose time

  • Uploading only pre-cut clips so there is no choice of opening
  • Passing feedback in pieces from several people
  • Redesigning the look mid-month
  • Sending files exported from a social app
  • Leaving every upload to the last week

Hire, freelance or subscribe

An Australian business thinking about editing usually has three options. Hiring someone adds wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision, which is hard to justify for 20 videos a month. A freelancer can be great for a single project but means a fresh brief each time.

The monthly package is a fixed fee with a repeatable process and no long-term commitment, so it can start small and be dropped if your plans change. The money side is on what short-form editing costs in Australia, and the buying side is on how to hire short-form editing in Australia.

An example of the rhythm, not a prediction of results.

Week one

Week two

FAQ

What footage can I send from Australia?

Raw camera footage, phone recordings, podcasts, screen recordings, UGC and older long-form videos all work.

Do you write the scripts?

No. 20CLIPS edits the footage you provide, reordering it for a stronger opening where needed. Scripts and filming are yours.

Does the time difference matter?

You upload when it suits you and review when the edits come back. The delivery schedule is agreed on the onboarding call.

Is the price in Australian dollars?

No. It is $999 a month in US dollars, for 20 videos.

Can one video go to several platforms?

Often. The package formats for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, with small changes to captions and pacing.

No. Sending footage in batches is the easiest way to use all 20.

Can you match my brand?

Yes. Send references and guidelines at the start.

How do I send big files?

Through the shared folder. Raw recordings are welcome.

Can I stop whenever I want?

Yes. There is no long-term commitment.
