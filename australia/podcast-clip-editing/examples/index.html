Clips that carry a conversation further

A good clip holds one idea that stands on its own and leaves people wanting more. Australian shows, from business interviews to sport and comedy, tend to have several such moments per episode. The editor finds them, and you decide the topics.

Hooks and layouts

Make a note of your favourite moments as you record. A timestamp takes a moment and saves the editor a lot of listening.

  • Begin on the guest's boldest sentence
  • Open on the question
  • Cut to the laugh, then back up
  • Lead with the conclusion
  • Put the guest's name and credential on screen

Layouts

  • Two shot
  • Single speaker
  • Captions only for audio
  • Waveform with captions

Working with remote guests

Many Australian shows record guests who are in another state. That is fine for clips, as long as each speaker has clear audio. If you can, ask guests to wear headphones so their voice does not bleed into the other microphone, and record locally as a backup.

Good audio is the cheapest improvement a show can make, and it lifts every clip drawn from the episode.

One look across the show

A consistent caption style and palette tie clips from different episodes together. References and brand files are collected at the start and reused.
