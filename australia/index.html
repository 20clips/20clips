Video editing services in Australia

Australian creators and businesses use short-form video to stay visible across a very large country. 20CLIPS does the editing as a remote service. You send raw footage to a shared folder, and 20 ready-to-publish short-form videos come back each month for a flat $999 in US dollars. They are formatted for Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, with captions, sound design and motion graphics included, so one set of footage can serve every platform an Australian audience uses.

How it works from Australia

It takes one 30-minute call to start. You explain what you make, share references and brand guidelines and agree a delivery schedule. After that, footage goes into the shared folder when you have it, and edited videos return for review with 2 revisions each and no long-term commitment.

Because it is remote, a city studio and a regional business follow the same steps, and nobody has to be in the same place or working the same hours. You upload when a shoot is done and review when the edits arrive.

If you are unsure where to begin, short-form video editing covers the whole package, and the narrower pages help once you know which platform matters most.

The price and what it covers

The fee is $999 a month in US dollars for 20 videos, roughly $50 each, with no per-video fees, no add-ons and no setup charge. You can cancel anytime, and if you need more than 20 videos you can raise it on the call. The complete list is on the pricing page.

The package edits footage you provide. Scripts, filming and posting stay with you, and a specific licensed track is yours to supply.

Who uses it across the country

Trades and service businesses use it for job showcases and quick explainers, coaches and trainers for recorded sessions, cafes and venues for dishes and atmosphere, and online stores for product and creator clips. Podcasters and creators use it to turn long recordings into a steady supply of short ones.

Video editing for creators, video editing for brands and video editing for agencies describe how each group works with it.

Employee, freelancer or subscription

Employing an editor in Australia brings wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision, which only pays off when video is a daily job. A freelancer suits a campaign but needs a new brief each time. The monthly package is one fee and one method, with nothing locking you in.
