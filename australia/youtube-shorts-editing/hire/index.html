Know your source and your volume

Cutting Shorts from long recordings depends on what you have and how many you want. A Gold Coast coach with weekly recorded sessions and a Hobart creator with a big back catalogue will land on different volumes. Fix the number before you compare suppliers.

Australian channels usually weigh a freelancer, an employee and a package service. The cost page explains the package price and the service page explains the work.

Six things to check

Moment selection

Ask how the editor finds a section that makes sense on its own.

Real reframing

A wide shot needs to be reframed around the speaker.

Strong openings

The first seconds decide a Short, so ask how hooks are built.

Accurate captions

They should follow speech and be easy to read.

Steady volume

Shorts reward regular posting.

Short commitment

Test the fit before signing for long.

Questions to put to each supplier

Ask every candidate the same list and compare written answers. If you already use suppliers in other cities, a shared-folder method is easy to adopt.

  • How do you choose moments from long videos?
  • How do you reframe a horizontal source?
  • How are captions styled?
  • How many Shorts each month?
  • How do revisions work?
  • Can the edits be adapted for other platforms?

Red flags

  • Clips that need context to make sense
  • Wide videos squeezed into vertical
  • No captions
  • Promises about subscribers

The routes compared

Freelancer

Good for a test batch. A regular channel means rebriefing and uneven style.

Employee

Good for daily video work. It brings wages, on-costs and equipment.

Monthly service

Good for a regular feed: 20 Shorts for $999, 2 revisions each and no lock-in.

Look at total monthly cost

A low quote per Short can conceal charges for captions, reframing and revisions, and a supplier you must rebrief costs hours that never reach an invoice. Add up a full month at the volume you want and compare totals.

If you often record a lot in one burst, check how each supplier copes. A fixed monthly volume spread across weeks tends to be simpler than paying per clip.

Use the first batch as a test

Check that each Short stands alone, the opening is strong, the reframing looks natural and the captions are accurate. If something is off, share two or three Shorts you admire and say why.

None of this depends on where you are. A regional operator and a city studio get the same process.
