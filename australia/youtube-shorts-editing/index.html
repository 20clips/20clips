The craft in a Short

What to look for in a finished one.

It makes sense alone

A good Short is one idea that needs no other context. The editor pulls forward what the viewer needs and drops what they do not.

The first line works hard

The opening shows the result, the surprising sentence or the question, with the set-up following.

The frame suits a phone

A wide source is reframed around the speaker or the action, not shrunk into a vertical box.

Captions do the carrying

Accurate, readable captions with a few emphasised words work with the sound off.

It leaves a trail

A good Short makes people curious about the rest of the channel. Links and descriptions stay with you.

How a month runs

A 30-minute call starts it. You describe your channel, say what you want Shorts to do and agree a delivery schedule that suits you.

You then upload long-form files, some Shorts whose style you like and your brand fonts and colours to a shared folder. If you already know your best moments, send timestamps; if not, the editor suggests candidates. Finished Shorts return for review with 2 revisions each, so changing the opening or trimming an ending is simple.

When approved, Shorts are ready to upload. If you are unsure how many Shorts a channel like yours should publish, this article on posting frequency helps.

Preparing recordings

Keep recording the long version. Even if you think a session was ordinary, a long file gives the editor a lot to choose from, and the best moment is often not the one you expected.

  • The full video or recording in the best quality you have
  • Timestamps of moments you like
  • Reference Shorts
  • Fonts, colours and logo
  • Lines or claims that must stay as spoken

Common slips

  • Cutting a clip without its set-up
  • Leaving a long intro in
  • Keeping wide framing
  • Posting whatever is left over
  • Posting once and stopping
