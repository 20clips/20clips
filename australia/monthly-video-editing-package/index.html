Common mistakes to avoid

Waiting to send footage until the end of the month

Videos you want published in week one cannot be edited if the footage arrives in week four. Send in batches. Australian teams that look back over the last month of posts before sending new footage tend to spot it first.

Treating the 20 videos as a quota to rush

Videos are best planned around what you want to say. Decide the topics first and let the footage follow. It is easy to correct while the batch is still being edited, which is why smaller, regular uploads work well.

Skipping the reference videos

References are the fastest way to get the style right. A few examples save revision rounds. Australian accounts that post to several platforms are especially prone to this, because posting quickly hides the problem.

Not using the revisions

Two revisions are included on each video. If something is off, ask. It is common among owner-operators in Australia, who film between jobs and have little time to plan.

Expecting scripting or filming

The package is editing. Writing scripts and filming footage stay with you. In competitive Australian categories this shows up fast, because viewers compare your video with the next one in the feed.

In-house, freelancer or a monthly package?

For an Australian business weighing the options, the real comparison is between a fixed monthly cost and several moving ones.

Editing in-house

A real option with enough constant work, but it adds salary, software, equipment and management for a job that comes at roughly 20 videos a month. Businesses in Australia that already pay for equipment and software often find the time cost is the one they underestimated.

Freelancers, one video at a time

You pay per project and re-brief each time, and the quality and style depend on who is free. An Australian hire brings salary, on-costs, equipment and management time, which is why many owners look at a remote monthly option first.

An agency retainer

Often includes strategy and filming as well as editing, which can be what you need but costs more than editing alone. A remote service suits Australian teams that want output they can plan around, without recruiting.
