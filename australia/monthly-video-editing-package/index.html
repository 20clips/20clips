Video on a subscription, without the surprises

How a month runs

You begin with a 30-minute call about what you make and where it is posted. You share reference videos and brand files, and agree how often footage will arrive. Turnaround and your delivery schedule are agreed there, based on your volume.

Each month then follows the same pattern. You upload footage to a shared folder, edited videos come back for review and you use your 2 revisions per video. Approved videos are finished files for you to publish. Many Australian clients send a batch after each shoot, such as a day on site or a recorded class, which spreads the work across the month.

Unused videos do not roll over, so sending footage in batches is the habit that matters. To choose a sensible volume, read how many short-form videos to post each week.

What the package leaves to you

The package edits what you send. Keeping the scope that tight is what keeps the price flat, and it means you know what a month will and will not include.

If you think you will need more than 20 videos, say so on the 30-minute call and it will be discussed before you commit.

Package, freelancer, employee or agency

Freelancers are flexible, but each project starts with a new brief and the result depends on who is free. Employing an editor brings wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision, which only pays off when video is a daily job well beyond 20 videos a month. An agency retainer often adds strategy and filming, which can be exactly what some businesses need and usually costs more than editing alone.
