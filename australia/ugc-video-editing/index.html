When your creators live in different cities

Australian online stores and brands rarely have their creators in one place. A fitness label might work with a trainer in Brisbane, a customer in Perth and a stylist in Melbourne, and each films on whatever phone and in whatever light they have. The clips that come back are authentic and inconsistent, with different framing, sound and pacing, and none of them is ready to go into an ad account or onto a feed.

20CLIPS edits that footage into a coherent set. You send the raw creator files and the brief. The editor builds hooks, adds captions, product overlays and b-roll, evens out the sound and returns 9:16 videos for organic posting or paid placements. The editor's checklist for every clip

Five things happen on every video.

Tidy, not staged

Sound is levelled, dead air removed and framing fixed, but the voice stays natural.

More than one hook

The editor builds alternative openings from the same clip, such as a problem, a result or a question. These count within your 20 videos.

Product on cue

Shots and overlays arrive as the creator mentions the item.

Captions for sound-off viewing

Text carries the message for people who do not turn the volume up.

Ready to use

Vertical files suited to organic channels and ad uploads.

How a month goes

Begin with a 30-minute call. You explain the products, who the creators are and where the videos will run, and agree a delivery schedule.

Creators send footage, and you drop it into a shared folder with a note of which brief it belongs to. With creators scattered across time zones, uploading as each one delivers keeps the work steady. Edited videos come back for review with 2 revisions each, so a changed hook or a corrected claim is a short note.

Approved videos are final files for organic posting or ad upload. For more on choosing a partner, read UGC video editing: what brands should look for.

What the editor needs

A short brief saves a round of revisions. The editor can make nearly anything look good, but cannot guess which promise matters to your business.

  • The original files from each creator
  • The brief and any mandatory lines
  • Product images, logos and disclaimers
  • Ads you want to resemble
  • Which angle each video should test

Avoidable delays

  • Over-polishing authentic clips
  • Only one opening tested
  • Forgetting that many viewers watch muted
  • Dropping the demonstration
  • Sending files without a brief

Employee, freelancer or monthly service

Asking creators to edit their own footage leads to uneven quality. An employee brings wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision, which is more than around 20 videos a month usually needs. A freelancer per video suits a campaign but needs briefing each time.
