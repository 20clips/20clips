How to hire UGC video editing in Australia: freelancer, in-house or monthly package

How to hire UGC video editing in Australia: what to look for, which questions to ask and how freelancers, employees and a monthly service compare.

A UGC editor has to keep the person in the picture

The trick with UGC is to make it cleaner and more consistent without it stopping feeling real. An editor who over-polishes removes the reason people trusted it, and one who under-edits leaves messy audio and slow openings. Before you start, work out how many videos you need and where they will run.

Australian brands normally choose between a freelancer, an employee and a package service. The cost page explains the package fee and the service page explains the work.

What to look for

Sound judgement on polish

Ask for examples where footage was cleaned up and kept its voice.

Variation skill

Ask how many openings can come from one clip.

Product timing

Overlays should land on the right words.

Upload-ready files

Videos should go straight into ad platforms.

One look from many creators

Different faces should still look like one brand.

Clear scope and terms

Know what is included and how to leave.

Questions to ask

Ask each candidate the same list and keep the written replies. A shared-folder method is easy for businesses already using suppliers in other cities.

  • How do you keep UGC authentic?
  • How do you deliver hook variations?
  • How do you time product overlays?
  • How do I send files from several creators?
  • How are revisions handled?
  • What formats do you deliver?

Red flags

  • Over-polished samples
  • No variations
  • Promises about ad results
  • A vague scope

The routes compared

Freelancer

Good for a single campaign. A rolling need means rebriefing and uneven style.

Employee

Good for daily creative testing, with wages, on-costs and equipment attached.

Monthly service

Good for steady creator footage: 20 videos for $999 and no lock-in.

Compare whole-month cost

A cheap per-video rate can hide extra charges for variations, overlays and revisions. Add up a month at the volume you plan and compare totals.

If your creators are spread around the country, ask how each supplier keeps one look across different people and different footage.

Treat batch one as the audit

Check whether clips still feel like a person, whether the hooks are different, whether overlays land on cue and whether files upload cleanly.

If the first batch misses, send a few ads you admire and explain what you liked.
