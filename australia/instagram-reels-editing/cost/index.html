Comparing the other ways to get Reels made

Editing Reels yourself costs nothing but time. Many owner-operators start strongly and stop when work picks up, which is why feeds go quiet.

Employing an editor adds wages, on-costs, equipment and supervision. For about 20 Reels a month it is usually more than the job needs.

Freelancers charge per project. That suits a one-off. A monthly feed means briefing again each time and accepting some drift in style. A subscription replaces both with a fixed fee, and the Australian hiring guide sets out the trade-offs.

Making 20 Reels go further

Film vertically if you can, and record clean sound. Both make every edit quicker.

Send whole sessions as well as short clips. A single recorded class or job walk-through can supply several Reels.

Settle your look early. Once your caption style is agreed, every later Reel is faster.

Not in the price

  • Writing post captions or scripts
  • Filming your clips
  • Scheduling or posting
  • Trending audio licences
  • More than 20 videos a month

A quick budgeting check for owner-operators

Work out what your time is worth for a week. If cutting and captioning a Reel takes you an hour and a half, twenty of them take a working week, and that week usually comes out of paid work. Set that against $999 and the question becomes less about cost and more about whether your Reels would exist at all without help.

It also helps to think about what a Reel is for. If one post a week brings in enquiries, 20 a month gives you a lot of chances to be seen. If your audience is small and local, you may be better off with a lighter plan, and the 30-minute call is the place to say so.

Terms worth knowing

The monthly fee is fixed and there is no setup charge. You can cancel anytime because there is no long-term commitment, so a one-month trial is a fair way to start. If you need more than 20 videos, mention it on the 30-minute call.
